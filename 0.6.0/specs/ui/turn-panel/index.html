    Turn panel¶

    The panel a turn happens on: the three piles face down, the die you roll, then the card you drew, turned over, beside the grid you work it out on.

    It replaces two dialogs. A turn used to cross roll and card, which showed the die, the pile and the card behind a Solve it button, and then the separate working-out grid. Now it is one panel in three phases:

    1. Ready: the three piles face down and the Roll button.
    2. Rolling: the die tumbles through 30 faces and settles on the face that was rolled.
    3. Solving: the drawn pile's card is picked up, turned over and laid across all three piles, showing the problem. The die goes, the working-out grid and keypad come in, and the card stays on screen until the answer is checked.

    Wireframed on #402. The layout, the header words and the timings below were agreed by Derek in an interactive prototype before this page was written. This page records those decisions, with the prototype's positions worked out again against the pile card's numbers so that every sum comes out exactly.

    How the spec is changing (#529). The first version of this page put a 405 px card beside the grid, and on a Hard card Help me's printed products had nowhere to go: they needed about 154 px left of the grid and there were 95. That was #529. Derek chose layout D from four drawn options: a smaller pile card, 200 × 360 instead of 225 × 405, and slimmer keypad keys, 124 px instead of 140. Together they free 93 px across, and the products now fit beside the grid at full size with the grid and the card untouched. The card's numbers are the pile card's and the keys are the working-out grid's; both pages say what changed. The sums below are layout D's.

    This page owns the panel: its regions, where they sit, the die, the Roll button, the phases and the motion between them. It does not own two things it shows:

    • The card itself. Its back, its front, its size and its pick-up and turn-over are the pile card's, and the PileCard* and RevealedCard* constants are referenced here, never restated with different values.
    • The grid and the keypad. Their columns, rows, carry strips, the growable addition section, Help me, the keypad and Check it are still the working-out grid's. The grid is now the Solving phase of this panel instead of a dialog of its own. Nothing inside it changed.

    Invariants¶

    Invariant: the roll happens in Core before anything of it is drawn on screen. Roll.Draw(Rng) picks the face from the game's single seeded Rng. The tumble is a readout of a result that already exists, never a source of one. No view code calls a random number generator to pick a face, including the 29 faces the tumble shows before the last one. Those come from Core too. Invariant: the face shown at the end of the tumble is the rolled face, and the card that is picked up and turned over is the card drawn from the pile that face maps to: 1 or 2 is Easy, 3 or 4 is Medium, 5 or 6 is Hard. Invariant: the panel cannot be dismissed, in any phase. Hardware back is inert. Once the board's Roll is pressed, the turn is under way. Invariant: nothing on the panel implies one pile is worth more than another. The three piles are drawn the same size, in the same style, with no score, star or bonus. They differ only in their pips and their name. The lime glow marks the pile that was drawn, never the best or hardest one. Invariant: the whole animation can be skipped by tapping anywhere. A tap at any moment from the Roll press until the panel has settled in Solving jumps straight to the settled Solving state. A four-player game plays this forty times, and by the fortieth nobody wants to watch it. Invariant: the tumble shows only faces that a real die could roll into next. Two faces in a row are never the same, and never opposite each other. Opposite faces add up to 7. Invariant: the two cards that were not drawn stay exactly where they are and are covered up. They do not fade, slide, shrink or unload. The turned-over card lies on top of them. Invariant: the drawn card stays on screen, face up, for the whole of Solving. The player can always see the problem they are working on and the pile it came from. Invariant: the grid is never made smaller to fit beside the card. If something does not fit at the working-out grid's own sizes, that is reported, not shrunk away. Everything fits: the widest card, with Help me pressed and all six products printed, overlaps nothing. Invariant: the grid does not move across when Help me is pressed. The room the printed products need on the grid's left is kept beside it from the moment it comes in, whether or not the button is ever pressed. (The grid does still re-centre down the panel when the button grows its addition section, which is the working-out grid's existing rule for a grown section.)

    Requirements¶

    Each is one behaviour, numbered so a build issue and its test can name it.

    • TP-1: The board's Roll opens the panel. Pressing Roll on the game board opens the turn panel in Ready. It opens the way every dialog opens (PS-8): a fade and a grow from DialogOpenScale, over DialogFadeDuration, over the DeepPond scrim. The panel covers the board.
    • TP-2: The panel's Roll starts the turn. Pressing the panel's own Roll button rolls the die in Core, draws the card from the pile the face maps to, and moves the panel to Rolling. The button fades out over TurnPanelRollFadeDuration and cannot be pressed again.
    • TP-3: The tumble. The die shows DieTumbleFaceCount faces, each held for DieTumbleStepDuration, in the order Core gives them. The last face is the rolled face. Every other face is a neighbour of the face after it: never the same face, and never its opposite. Core builds the sequence backwards from the rolled face, using the game's seeded Rng.
    • TP-4: The jiggle. While it tumbles, the die is turned DieTumbleJiggle clockwise on the first face, the same anticlockwise on the second, and so on alternately. On the last face it is turned 0°.
    • TP-5: The match. When the last face lands, the die rests for DieSettleHold. At the same moment a Lime glow fades in around the drawn pile's card, so the link from the die to the pile is seen before the card moves.
    • TP-6: The reveal. After DieSettleHold the drawn card is picked up and turned over exactly as the pile card specifies (PC-6, then PC-7). The glow goes with the card as it lifts, and fades out PileGlowOffDelay into the turn-over.
    • TP-7: The phase reveal. When the card lands, over PhaseRevealDuration, the die fades out where it stands and the grid and keypad fade in where they stand. Nothing slides. At the start of that fade the header's words change from Roll the die to Work it out, and the skip hint fades out.
    • TP-8: Skip. A tap anywhere on the screen during Rolling, or during the move into Solving, jumps straight to the settled Solving state:
    • the die is gone;
    • the drawn card is turned over in its final place;
    • the other two cards are where they always were;
    • the glow is off;
    • the grid and keypad are in;
    • the words say Work it out;
    • the hint is gone.

    The tap that skips does nothing else. It does not reach the grid or the keypad underneath. While the animation runs, the hint Tap anywhere to skip sits at the bottom right. - TP-9: Back is inert. Hardware back does nothing in any phase.

    Regions¶

    Each region keeps one name in every phase it appears in.

    Region Job Ready Rolling Solving

    piles The three pile cards face down, and then the drawn card turned over on top of them Three backs Three backs; the drawn one glows at the end The drawn card's front, covering all three
    die The one die Resting on 1 Tumbling, then resting on the rolled face Faded out
    roll The panel's Roll button Shown Faded out —

    die and roll together are the stage: the space to the right of piles in Ready and Rolling. grid and keypad take that same space in Solving.

    Anchors¶

    The panel is the full-bleed dialog: TurnPanelWidth × TurnPanelHeight, which is the canvas inset by 48 px on every side, centred on the canvas over the scrim. Every position on this page is measured from the panel's outer edge, outline included, the same way the pile card measures its own numbers. The content runs from DialogPadding (56 px) inside the left edge to DialogPadding inside the right edge: 1824 − 2 × 56 = 1712 px.

    • header is the working-out grid's header band, unchanged and in place for all three phases. The chip is at the content's left edge, GridHeaderTop (44 px) down. The words follow it, GridHeaderGap (32 px) after the chip, so they move with the length of the name. Help me is pinned to the content's right edge, in Solving only. The band ends GridHeaderHeight (140 px) below the panel's top.
    • piles is pinned to the content's left edge, TurnPanelPileTop down. This is the same 216 px as KeypadTop, so the top of the cards lines up with the top of the keypad in Solving. Its footprint is the pile card's: in the chosen layout, a column PileCardLongSide wide and 3 × PileCardShortSide + 2 × PileCardGap tall, 360 × 648. The cards sit PileCardGap (24 px) apart, Easy at the top, Medium, then Hard.
    • The stage runs from the right edge of piles to the content's right edge. die and roll are centred in it across, one above the other, TurnPanelDieRollGap apart. As a group they are centred down the height of piles.
    • keypad is the grid page's. It is pinned to the content's right edge, KeypadTop (216 px) down, KeypadWidth (404 px) wide, with Check it KeypadSubmitGap below it. Its keys are KeypadKeySize (124 px), slimmer than the 140 px they were, for layout D.
    • grid is centred in the space left over, together with the room its printed products need. Across, that space runs from the right edge of piles to the keypad's left edge, 948 px. What is centred in it is one block: GridHelpColumnWidth (128 px) for the help column, GridHelpGap (32 px), then the grid. The block is the same whether or not Help me has been pressed, so the grid's left edge is too. Down, the grid alone is centred from the bottom of the header band to the panel's last usable pixel, unchanged at 908 px.
    • help is the grid page's right-aligned column, its right edge GridHelpGap left of the grid. On a Hard card its widest line, 40 × 300, is 122.5 px, inside the 128 px kept for it.
    • hint has its right edge at the content's right edge and its bottom TurnPanelSkipHintBottom above the panel's bottom edge. That is in the panel's padding, below everything else. The hint only shows while the stage is empty below the die.

    Where an odd pixel has to be split, it goes right, or down. Centring an even width in an odd space, or an odd width in an even one, leaves one pixel over. Rather than place anything on a half pixel, the space before the thing is rounded down and the space after it takes the remainder. The code centres the same way, with integer division.

    On a screen that is not 1920 × 1200, nothing here moves. The panel is laid out in the reference canvas like every dialog (what fills a screen that is not 16:10), and the scrim reaches the screen's edges.

    What fits beside the card¶

    Everything, at full size. The widest card's grid is 664 px, the widest product is 122.5 px, and with GridHelpGap between them they need 818 of the 948 px between the card and the keypad. Nothing in the grid was shrunk, moved inside, or changed size to make room for the card.

    It did not fit at first. Beside a 405 px card, with 140 px keys, the grid had 855 px and was centred in it, leaving 95 px on its left, and the product column needs 154. Derek chose layout D on #529: the card loses 45 px across (405 → 360) and the keypad loses 48 (452 → 404), which gives the grid's space 93 px more, 855 → 948. He chose it from four drawn layouts. The products, GridHelpGap, the grid and the card's design all keep their sizes; only the card's scale and the keys changed.

    Named constants¶

    Panel geometry and timing. The pile card's numbers are the pile card's. The grid's and the keypad's are the working-out grid's. Neither is restated here.

    The panel¶

    Element Constant Value
    Panel width TurnPanelWidth 1824 px, which is DialogMaxWidth
    Panel height TurnPanelHeight 1104 px, which is DialogMaxHeight
    Padding DialogPadding 56 px (shared)
    The piles' top, down from the panel's top edge TurnPanelPileTop 216 px, the same as KeypadTop
    Gap between the three face-down cards PileCardGap 24 px. The pile card owns it; see below

    The issue's PileRowGap is not declared. The gap between the cards is PileCardGap. That is the G in L² = 3S² + 2GS, so it cannot change without changing the card. A second name for the same 24 px is how the two would drift apart.

    The timing¶

    Element Constant Value
    Faces the tumble shows, the last being the rolled face DieTumbleFaceCount 30
    How long each face is held DieTumbleStepDuration 0.04 s (40 ms), so 1.2 s in all
    The die's turn on alternate faces DieTumbleJiggle 8°, clockwise and anticlockwise in turn; 0° on the last
    The die resting on the rolled face, the pile glowing DieSettleHold 0.35 s
    Pick-up, hold, turn-over PileCardLiftDuration, PileCardLiftHold, PileCardRevealDuration 0.3 s, 0.15 s, 0.7 s (the pile card's)
    Die out, grid and keypad in PhaseRevealDuration 0.25 s

    Why 40 ms. Thirty faces at 40 ms is 1.2 s, which is the same length as the old dialog's whole entry. At 25 faces a second each face is still seen as a face rather than a blur, and the turn changes are fast enough to read as a die tumbling rather than a slideshow. It is short enough to sit through on most turns of a four-player game. The ones nobody wants to watch are what skip is for. Derek agreed it in the prototype.

    The whole sequence, from the panel's Roll:

    From To What happens
    0 0.2 s Roll fades out; the hint fades in
    0 1.2 s 30 faces, 40 ms each, jiggling
    1.2 s 1.55 s The die rests on the rolled face; the glow fades in
    1.55 s 1.85 s The drawn card is picked up
    1.85 s 2.0 s It rests, picked up
    2.0 s 2.7 s It turns over and lands on the three piles; the glow fades out from 2.245 s
    2.7 s 2.95 s The die fades out, the grid and keypad fade in, the words change, the hint fades out

    About three seconds, end to end, or one tap.

    Elements¶

    • Player chip: the active dialog chip, showing the player's frog and name, in every phase. Four players share the tablet, and the board behind is dimmed, so the panel says whose turn it is.
    • Roll the die / Work it out: the words beside the chip. They say what to do now. Roll the die shows in Ready and Rolling, and changes to Work it out once the card is turned over. The old word rolled is gone. It made the chip into a sentence about something that had already happened. The panel now opens before the roll, so the words tell you what to do next instead.
    • Pile cards × 3: pile cards, Easy, Medium and Hard, face down. They are not buttons. The roll picks the card, and the player never does.
    • Die: one six-sided die, drawn with pips, never a numeral. It is not a button either.
    • Roll: the panel's primary button. Pressing it rolls; see TP-2. This is a second press after the board's Roll, on purpose: the board's opens the panel, and this one throws the die.
    • The revealed card: the drawn card's front, carrying the problem and, on its small log, the pile's name. The drawn pile is named in words for the whole of Solving. The old grid showed it only in its readout pill.
    • Working-out grid, keypad, Check it, Help me: exactly as the working-out grid specifies. Check it is disabled until there is a digit in the answer row, so it arrives disabled.
    • Tap anywhere to skip: words, not a button. The whole screen is the thing to tap.

    The grid's card readout pill is gone. It used to show 331 × 41 · hard pile in the header, because the grid dialog had no other way to show the card. The card itself is now on screen beside the grid, bigger, and named, so the pill would say the same thing twice. The prototype Derek agreed has no pill.

    Behaviour¶

    • Opening. Roll on the board opens the panel in Ready. The panel fades and grows in like every dialog. The scrim covers the board, and the board underneath does not move.
    • Ready. The three piles are face down, the die rests on 1, and Roll is live. Nothing has been rolled yet, and nothing can be dismissed: back does nothing, and there is no close. Tapping anywhere but Roll does nothing.
    • Ready → Rolling. Roll is pressed. Core rolls the die and draws the card, then hands the view the rolled face, the drawn card and the tumble's 30 faces. Only then does anything move. Roll fades out over TurnPanelRollFadeDuration, the hint fades in, and the die starts its tumble where it stands. Nothing moves across the panel.
    • Rolling. The die tumbles through DieTumbleFaceCount faces at DieTumbleStepDuration, jiggling, and lands unturned on the rolled face. It rests for DieSettleHold while the drawn pile's card glows.
    • Rolling → Solving. Three things happen in order:
    • The card. It is picked up (PileCardLiftDuration), rests (PileCardLiftHold), and turns over onto the three piles (PileCardRevealDuration), exactly as the pile card says. The glow lifts with it and fades out PileGlowOffDelay into the turn-over.
    • The card lands. The other two cards have not moved; they are underneath it.
    • The phase reveal. Over PhaseRevealDuration the die fades out where it stands, the grid and keypad fade in where they will be used, the words change to Work it out, and the hint fades out.

    Nothing is removed by sliding, and nothing moves except the drawn card. - Solving. The working-out grid's behaviour, unchanged, with the caret in the answer row's leftmost box. The card stays. Check it goes to answer result, and that is how the panel is left. - Skip. See TP-8. The skip goes from wherever the animation has got to, straight to the settled Solving state, with no animation of its own. - Back does nothing, in every phase.

    How the phases line up with the turn¶

    Phase On screen TurnPhase
    Ready Three face-down cards, the die on 1, Roll WaitingToRoll
    Rolling The die tumbling, then resting; the glow RolledAndCardDrawn
    Solving The card being turned over, then the grid and keypad Answering

    The panel moves forward one phase at a time and never back, the way TurnPhase does.

    What the corners do¶

    The turned-over card's corner is PileCardRadius × 9/5 = 46.8 px, and the undrawn cards underneath it have 26 px corners. Where the front covers a card in a corner position, a sliver of that card's corner shows past the rounder front, like a card lying on a pile. That is what the two radii give. The prototype shows it too, and nothing here hides it.

    The two pictures differ only where the piles force them to. The header, panel, die, Roll and every size are the same. The row's sum is the pile card's: 3 × 125 + 2 × 15 = 405.

    Nothing about above is built unless it is chosen after all. If it is, its Solving layout still has to be agreed, and so does the pile card's landscape front.

    Open questions¶

    None is open. The one this page raised is answered:
